# [Anti-Interference]Precoding for Anti-Interference Attack Based on DIRS with Full Passive Interference Mitigation[Attached MATLAB Code]

The emerging intelligent reflective surface (IRS) significantly enhances system performance but also poses a significant risk to physical layer security. Existing research indicates that Disco-like IRSs, characterized by random time-varying reflection properties, can be exploited by attackers to actively age legitimate users' (LU) channels. This active channel aging (ACA) generated by DIRS does not rely on interference power or LU channel state information (CSI), and can interfere with multi-user multiple-input single-output (MU-MISO) systems. To address the significant threat posed by full-passive jammers (FPJs) based on DIRS, this paper proposes an anti-jamming precoding scheme that requires only the statistical characteristics of the ACA channel derived from DIRS, rather than its CSI. The statistical characteristics of the DIRS jamming channel are first derived, followed by the design of the anti-jamming precoding scheme based on these statistical characteristics. Furthermore, this paper demonstrates that the proposed anti-jamming precoding scheme can achieve maximum signal-to-interference plus noise ratio (SJNR). To obtain the ACA statistical characteristics without altering the system architecture or colluding with illegal DIRS, a data frame structure is designed such that legitimate access points (APs) can estimate these statistical characteristics. During the design of the data frame, when a LU detects an interference attack, it simply needs to report its received power to the legitimate AP. Numerical results validate the effectiveness of the proposed anti-jamming precoding scheme against FPJs based on DIRS, as well as the feasibility of the designed data frame for legitimate APs to estimate statistical characteristics.
Introduction: Wireless communication is vulnerable to eavesdropping and interference attacks due to its broadcast and superposition characteristics. To protect legitimate users from eavesdropping, encryption techniques are used to prevent interceptors from capturing transmitted signals. Since encryption relies on the computational difficulty of breaking the underlying mathematical processes required to crack the password, only those with strong computational capabilities can effectively receive the transmitted signals.
On the other hand, jamming attacks (also known as denial-of-service attacks) can be initiated by active jammers (AJs), which intentionally interfere with the communication between legitimate access points (APs) and their legitimate users. In practice, physical layer active jammers can generally be categorized into constant jammers, intermittent jammers, reactive jammers, and adaptive jammers. Constant jammers continuously broadcast interference signals (such as modulated Gaussian waves or pseudorandom noise) on open wireless channels to block legitimate users from communicating with the legitimate AP. However, constant jammers have low energy efficiency because they continuously consume power, a fundamental disadvantage of active jammers. To overcome this drawback, researchers have investigated intermittent jammers, reactive jammers, and adaptive jammers. The basic idea behind these active jammers is to reduce the duration of interference transmission to lower power consumption. However, all types of active jammers require some interference power to effectively attack legitimate users. Given the inherent energy disadvantage of active jammers, can interference attacks be launched without using interference power?
In recent years, intelligent reflectors (IRS) have been considered a promising technology for future 6G systems, capable of reflecting electromagnetic waves in a controlled manner. Specifically, IRS is a super thin surface equipped with multiple subwavelength reflective elements whose electromagnetic response (i.e., amplitude and phase shift) can be controlled through simple programmable PINs or varactor diodes. Previous research has mainly focused on the use of IRS to improve performance metrics such as spectral efficiency (SE), energy efficiency (EE), cell coverage, or physical layer security, assuming that the legitimate AP knows the channel state information (CSI) related to the IRS and can control its phase response. Studies have significantly enhanced coverage and spectral efficiency through the actual design of reconfigurable surfaces.
However, the emergence of IRS also poses a serious potential threat to wireless networks. Some studies have indicated that illegal IRS can significantly impact wireless networks due to their passive nature, which makes them difficult to detect. For example, there has been research on an adversarial IRS-based passive jammer, which essentially does not consume interference power and can minimize the received power (i.e., signal-to-noise ratio) at legitimate users by combining reflected signals with direct path signals through phase cancellation. Although this passive jammer can initiate jamming attacks without using interference power, unauthorized IRS must know all the channel state information (CSI). Due to the passive nature of IRS, the CSI of IRS-aided channels is jointly estimated with the CSI of legitimate APs and legitimate users. Specifically, leveraging the reciprocity of time-division duplex (TDD) channels, PUs send pilot signals to APs instead of legitimate IRSs, and then the AP estimates the IRS-aided channel using methods such as least squares (LS) estimation. If an illegal IRS aims to acquire CSI from legitimate users, it must train and learn CSI in conjunction with legitimate APs and legitimate users. Therefore, the assumption that "an illegal IRS knows CSI" is not realistic in real-world wireless networks.
Considering the difficulty of illegal IRS (IRS) acquisition for CSI, can an interference attack be initiated without using jamming power or obtaining legitimate user CSI? A novel full-passive jammer (FPJ) has been proposed that utilizes a Disco IRS (DIRS) with random phase shifts to actively age legitimate users' channels, thereby launching an interference attack against legitimate users without either legitimate user CSI or jamming power. This results in severe active channel aging (ACA) interference, a type of inter-user interference (IUI). Specifically, DIRS controllers in existing studies randomly generate a reflection vector during the reverse frequency translation (RFT) stage and then another reflection vector during the data transmission (DT) stage. Further research indicates that ACA interference based on DIRS can also be introduced by shutting down the illegal IRS during the RFT stage and generating multiple random reflection vectors during the DT stage. This time-based FPJ based on DIRS must know when the RFT stage ends and when the DT stage begins, requiring some form of synchronization. These DIRS-based FPJs are difficult to detect due to their passive nature, posing significant risks to physical layer security. In traditional wireless networks, classic interference mitigation methods such as spread spectrum and frequency hopping have been widely used to suppress interference attacks. Spread spectrum refers to the expansion of signal energy over a frequency range wider than the minimum required for transmission. Additionally, frequency hopping is a technique employed in spread spectrum communication where the carrier frequency changes rapidly at both transmitter and receiver known patterns. However, these classical interference mitigation methods cannot be applied to counter FPJ, as the source of interference attacks initiated by FPJ comes from legitimate AP transmission signals themselves, which share the same characteristics (e.g., carrier frequency).
Moreover, ACA interference generated by FPJ based on DIRS cannot be alleviated using multi-input multi-output (MIMO) interference cancellation techniques. MIMO interference cancellation is only effective when the legitimate AP knows the channel information of the legitimate user and the DIRS interference channel. However, the phase shifts and amplitudes of DIRS are randomly generated. Studies have shown that with an adequate number of DIRS components, a single-bit quantized phase shift based FPJ can achieve the expected interference effect. The key advantage of these methods is that there currently exists no effective anti-interference method to counteract these destructive interference attacks imposed by FPJ based on DIRS.
To address the significant risk posed by illegal IRS, this paper proposes an anti-interference precoding scheme for FPJ attacks based on DIRS, which only requires statistical characteristics of the DIRS-based ACA channel, not its instantaneous CSI. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
Considering a practical IRS model where the phase shift of DIRS reflective elements is discrete and amplitude is a function of its corresponding phase shift, this paper describes a persistent FPJ based on DIRS that initiates jamming attacks through ACA interference without requiring additional jamming power or legitimate user CSI knowledge. The DIRS phase shift is randomly generated once during the RPT stage and multiple times during the DT stage. Compared to related research, the persistent FPJ model based on DIRS is more harmful because it does not need to be synchronized with the training process of the legitimate system. Therefore, the developed anti-jamming precoder is more comprehensive. Furthermore, the persistent FPJ based on DIRS in this study has different characteristics compared to the FPJ in related studies, such as the distribution of random DIRS phase shifts affects the interference impact of the persistent FPJ based on DIRS.
To counter the severe threat brought by FPJ based on DIRS, this paper develops an anti-jamming precoder that only requires the statistical characteristics of the DIRS ACA channel without needing to acquire real-time CSI (acquiring real-time CSI is impractical). Firstly, the statistical characteristics of the DIRS ACA channel for both persistent FPJ and time-based FPJ in related studies are derived. Based on these derived characteristics, the differences between the two methods' interference impacts are explained. Secondly, an anti-jamming precoder is developed solely based on statistical characteristics and proven to achieve maximum SJNR. The proposed anti-jamming strategy is applicable to both persistent FPJ and the Disco method in related studies.
For practical applications, legal APs must obtain ACA statistical characteristics without altering the system architecture or colluding with illegal DIRS. To this end, a data frame structure was designed for legal APs to estimate statistical characteristics. Specifically, when a legal user detects interference, it only needs to feedback its received power to the legal AP. This requires minimal overhead, as the received power value of a legal user is scalar and can be effectively estimated with just a few feedback transmissions.

Conclusions This study investigates the significant threats brought by FPJ based on DIRS. To address this, we developed a novel anti-interference precoding scheme that only utilizes the statistical characteristics of the DIRS-based interference channel without requiring real-time CSI. Our theoretical analysis and numerical results lead to the following conclusions, which raise concerns about the significant physical risks associated with FPJ based on DIRS.
The FPJ based on DIRS initiates interference attacks by introducing multi-user ACA interference generated by DIRS, allowing it to interfere with legitimate users without using the interference power or understanding the CSI of the legitimate user. Increasing the transmitting power of the legitimate AP does not reduce the interference impact of the FPJ based on DIRS but actually makes it more harmful. Additionally, the FPJ based on DIRS can circumvent existing anti-interference technologies such as spread spectrum, hopping frequency, and MIMO interference cancellation.
The elements of the ACA channel based on DIRS follow a zero mean, variance Gaussian distribution. Since the gain values of each DIRS reflective element are functions of its corresponding phase shift, their variance is related to the distribution of random DIRS phase shifts. The interference impact of persistent FPJ based on DIRS is more severe than that of time-based FPJ based on DIRS. Moreover, persistent FPJ based on DIRS do not require synchronization. Therefore, for MU-MISO systems, persistent FPJ based on DIRS is more harmful than time-based FPJ based on DIRS.
Based on the derived ACA channel distribution, an anti-interference precoder is proposed, which achieves maximum SJNR. Particularly, for MU-MISO systems operating at low transmission power, the proposed anti-interference precoder not only prevents persistent FPJ and time-based FPJ from interfering with legitimate users but also improves the SJNR of legitimate users due to the additional channel paths provided by them. To obtain statistical characteristics in practice, a data frame structure is designed for legitimate APs to estimate statistical characteristics, which only requires legitimate users to feedback once or twice their received power to the legitimate AP.
